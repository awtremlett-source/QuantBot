## In plain words

- **Why the luck bar was wrong (two lines).** The bench measured "luck" with coin-flip
  portfolios that hold every share half the time, even shares the real portfolio is not
  allowed to hold. One of those (SMEA.L) has a broken price on 27 November 2023 — a 115-fold
  "jump" where the data feed switched units — so half the coin portfolios booked a fake
  +344% day and half did not, and the luck bar came out far too high.
- **The box's first guess is rejected:** different cost bills explain under 1% of the
  spread; that one broken night explains 99.9%. Costs only stretch it (the gap doubles at
  double costs).
- **The box's fix is the right kind of fix.** Random portfolios built by the real rules
  (random rankings, same slots, buffer, stops and costs) cannot hold SMEA.L that month — it
  never passed the rules' 90% data-coverage test in 2023 — and none of the 100 random-rank books did.
  Their spread is tiny (0.005 at 1× and at 2×, against 0.072 / 0.153 for the coins), so the luck
  bar would be about 0.012, far below the planted winner's 0.21–0.24.
- **But we stopped before building it (box rule: a defect).** Checking every price the six
  rules read found a second broken stretch that the real portfolio CAN hold: EDV.L's 3½ years
  of mostly frozen prices before it listed in London, ending in a fake −41% day (three of the
  QT-19 test portfolios held it into that day). Running the six rules now would spend them on
  known-bad prices, and each rule can only be judged once.
- **Does the bench now pass the planted winner and still fail random picks? Not tested** (not
  re-run). The measurements say it should, once the prices are fixed.
- **The six rules: none was run, none failed.** Trial count stays 42. **The advisor's honest
  default stays "hold the index fund"; S9 stays open.**
- **QT-21, in order:** quarantine the broken rows through the one front door (kept, never
  deleted) with a check that would have caught them; build the box's fix exactly as it was
  specified; re-prove the bench both ways; then run the six rules as registered.

**What explains the coin spread (raw data; 1× and 2× give the same shares):**

| | share of the spread |
|---|---|
| (a) cost bill / turnover differences between coins | 1.1% |
| (b) market exposure (beta to D3, pot exposure) | 86% on its own, but that is the broken day bending each coin's beta estimate (1.81 vs 1.90); added after (c): 0.01% |

The coins fall into two tight groups: held SMEA.L into that open (102 coins: Sharpe −0.046,
sd 0.0007, kurtosis ≈ 4,750 — that one bar is ~90% of all their variance) or not (98 coins:
Sharpe −0.190, sd 0.003). At 2× the two groups sit at −0.121 and −0.427.

**A2 — diagnosis.** *Hypothesis "cost noise": rejected* — every coin pays nearly the same
bill (182% of the pot a year at 1×, sd 0.3 points); it explains under 1% of the spread. The
spread is one broken price that only the coin can reach. SMEA.L's open on 2023-11-27 is
114.6× its previous close (a unit break in the feed). The coin ignores the book's
eligibility, so 102 of 200 coins held SMEA.L into that open and booked +344% of the pot in one
bar, tripling their volatility and pulling their Sharpe from −0.19 to −0.05. The book itself
can never hold it then: SMEA.L's 90%-coverage test fails at every 2023 month-end
(0.81–0.87). Costs are the amplifier, not the cause: daily churn puts every coin's Sharpe
far below zero, the jump removes about three quarters of that distance, so the gap between
the groups grows with the cost level — 0.071 at 1× became 0.151 at 2× in QT-19.

**Cross-check (ii), analytic.** One coin book's own Sharpe noise from its moments: 0.014 / 0.016
(1× / 2×) on clean data; 0.033 / 0.067 for a coin holding the jump (skew 66, kurtosis
≈ 4,750) — the same broken bar, seen from inside one book.

- **(i) and (ii) disagree by more than 2× (≈ 0.012 vs ≈ 0.031–0.036), as expected.** The nulls
  measure how far skill-free books sit from **each other** on the one price history they
  share (the market's luck is common to all); (ii) measures how far **one** book's Sharpe
  sits from its true value by the luck of that history — which the PSR already charges in
  its own denominator. SR0 is about the first.
- **A question for the mentor (not acted on):** the Deflated Sharpe centres the
  "best of N lucky tries" bar on zero. A long-only book earns the market's Sharpe with no
  skill (random-rank books: +0.016 per bar at 1×, -0.008 at 2×), so centring on the
  skill-free books' own average would be stricter at 1× and looser at 2×. Today the luck test
  and "beat D3" are what stop a market-riding book.
