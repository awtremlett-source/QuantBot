## In plain words

- **A trailing stop** is a "sell if it falls this far" line that follows a share up and never comes
  down. Batch 1's line sat 3 × ATR below the price (ATR = the average daily range; about 6% on
  these shares), and it sold 40–43% of all buys within a month.
- **The big-fall stop** (your GO) puts that line **15% below the highest price since the buy** —
  10% while markets are jumpy (VIX, Wall Street's "fear gauge", above 30, or no reading). Both are
  the figures S5's crash tests (QT-16) fixed: 15% is the fall that fired in the 2008 and 2020
  crashes on every index replayed (and 2022 on three of four); 10% is where the brake tightens
  stops. **Every holding still has a stop**; it still only moves up; a close below it still sells
  at the next morning's open.
- **Batch 2 = batch 1's six rules, only the stop changed**, so any difference is the stop's.
  Names A2-01..A2-06 (A2-01 = A-01's rule, and so on). Trial count **48 → 54**.
- **The stop was most of the damage.** After double costs, against the index fund's **+265%** over
  the same 11½ years (2014-03 → 2025-10), every rule gained 112–250 points:
  - A2-01 momentum (12 months): +24.7% → **+256.8%** · A2-02 momentum (6 months): +12.4% → **+197.0%**
  - A2-03 smooth momentum: +34.8% → **+284.6%** — *more than the index fund* · A2-04 near the
    52-week high: +5.4% → **+143.2%** · A2-05 calmest shares: +2.1% → **+152.1%** · A2-06 momentum
    with a market filter: +19.5% → **+131.3%**
- **None survived — not even A2-03.** It beat the index fund, but the **luck test** failed: the
  same shares, held for the same lengths but bought at random times, did about as well (p 0.74; a
  pass needs under 0.05). So the rule's timing shows no skill, and a 19-point lead over 11½ years
  is within what luck gives here. All six failed the luck test.
- **So the advisor's honest default stays "hold the index fund"; S9 stays open.** No holdout was
  opened (none passed).
