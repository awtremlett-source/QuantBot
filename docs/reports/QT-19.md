## In plain words

- **What the six rules try** (written down and locked before any code existed):
  - A-01: buy the shares that rose most over the last year, ignoring the latest month.
  - A-02: the same over the last six months.
  - A-03: A-01, but a smooth rise counts for more than a jumpy one.
  - A-04: buy the shares trading closest to their highest price of the past year.
  - A-05: buy the calmest shares (the smallest day-to-day swings).
  - A-06: A-01, but only while the world index fund is above its 200-day average
    (otherwise hold cash).

  Each month the advisor would hold the best fifth of its 171 shares, equal amounts,
  each with its trailing stop.
- **None of the six was run, and none of them failed.** Before any rule is judged,
  the test bench must prove it can tell a real edge from luck on this kind of
  portfolio (the "known-null" check). It could not. We hid a strong, real pattern in
  copies of the price data. After double costs it turned the money into about 337
  times the start, against the index fund's +265%. The bench still said FAIL.
- **Why:** one of the bench's tests, the *Deflated Sharpe*, asks whether a result
  beats the best of many tries by pure luck. It measures "luck" with random
  coin-flip portfolios. On this fully invested monthly portfolio those random
  portfolios spread out 5 to 10 times more than chance alone explains, so the luck
  bar was set impossibly high. A bench that fails a planted winner cannot be trusted
  to judge the real rules. The box's rule is to **stop**, and we stopped.
- **So the advisor's honest default stays "hold the index fund"**, until a rule
  survives a bench that has proved itself. S9 stays open. Nothing was lost: the six
  rules are registered and unchanged, so QT-20 can run them exactly as written once
  the bench is fixed.
- Two things learned on the way, both for QT-20:
  1. **The trailing stop is tight for monthly holding.** At 3× the daily average
     range, it sells about half of all buys within the month (random picks:
     turnover about 10× a year).
  2. **The share list flatters history.** It was chosen in 2026 from today's big,
     busy shares, so it only contains companies that survived and grew. Momentum
     rules would look better here than they would have in real life, and the 1% a
     year mark-down is only a starting figure.
