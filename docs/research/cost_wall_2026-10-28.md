# The cost wall, described — 2026-10-28 (QT-27 C)

> **Description only — choosing a sleeve from it is a NEW trial.** Nothing here was used to build or tune the
> combined model (registered first, in commit `6be96dad`, before this page was made); nothing here is counted
> in the trial log. Unsealed data only (1-hour bars before 2026-07-13, daily bars before 2025-10-10).

## In plain words

- **What a round trip costs.** Buying and then selling £300 of something costs, at our normal (1×) figures:
  **0.20%** for a London ETF, **0.40%** for a US share (mostly the 0.15% currency fee each way) and **0.70%** for a
  London share (mostly the 0.5% stamp duty on the buy). The bot is judged at double those (2×): 0.40%, 0.80%, 1.40%.
- **How far prices move in the hours the bot could hold.** From batch 2's buy (bar 3's open) to the forced sale
  (the last hour before the close), the typical move — up or down — is **0.31%** for London ETFs, **0.60%** for
  US shares and **0.56%** for London shares. For London ETFs and London shares the typical move is **smaller than
  the 2× cost**; for US shares it is a little under it too.
- **How often a day clears the wall.** About **1 day in 5** rises by more than the 2× cost for London ETFs and US
  shares — and about the same share falls by more than it. For London shares only **1 day in 15** rises past
  1.4%. To make money the bot has to pick those days in advance, much better than chance, every time it trades.
- **What a same-day bot can never earn.** Over 2014–2025, the world index fund (VWRL) gained **+272%**, dividends
  included. **All of it came overnight** (from one day's close to the next day's open: **+797%**). Holding only
  between the open and the close of each day **lost 59%**. VWRP (the same fund, dividends kept inside) says the
  same from 2019: overnight +219%, daytime −39%, total +95%. A bot that must be flat every night (P4) stands
  in the daytime half only.

## C1. Per sleeve — cost against the move it must beat (2024-02-01 → 2026-07-10)

Move = open of E (the session's first bar the P4 cut forces flat) ÷ open of the fill bar (two bars after bar 1,
batch 2's decision bar) − 1, per name-day, own currency, no stop. Name-days counted only where bar 1 starts at the
exchange's open and the fill lands before E (batch 2's own skip rules, P15 not applied: it changes no price).

| sleeve | names | name-days | round trip 1× | round trip 2× | median \|move\| | mean move | rose > 2× cost | fell > 2× cost | rose > 1× cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uk_etf | 9 | 5,260 | 0.20% | 0.40% | 0.31% | +0.002% | 20.4% | 19.5% | 33.3% |
| us_liquid | 30 | 17,713 | 0.40% | 0.80% | 0.60% | +0.034% | 20.0% | 19.0% | 33.2% |
| uk_share | 10 | 6,160 | 0.70% | 1.40% | 0.56% | +0.011% | 6.5% | 7.2% | 20.7% |

- uk_etf has 9 names: VALL.L has no unsealed 1-hour bars (as in batch 1).
- The rises and falls past the wall are almost equal in number, and the mean move is close to zero: the hours
  between the fill and the cut carry no free drift to lean on. Clearing the wall is a question of picking days.
- Costs from qb2/execution/costs.py at £300 (costs.round_trip), the one cost model.

## C2. VWRL daily, 2014-03-31 → 2025-10-09 — overnight against daytime

| line | dates | bars | total | overnight only (close → next open) | daytime only (open → close) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VWRL.L (dividends credited overnight, at the ex-date's open) | 2014-03-31 → 2025-10-09 | 2,743 | **+271.6%** | **+797.4%** | **−58.6%** |
| VWRP.L (accumulating; from its first stored bar) | 2019-08-13 → 2025-10-09 | 1,439 | **+94.5%** | **+219.3%** | **−39.1%** |

- The box asked for VWRP from 2014-03-31; VWRP's stored history starts 2019-08-13, so the full span is measured
  on its distributing twin VWRL (the same index), with its 46 OK dividends credited to the overnight leg — the
  holder at the ex-date's open is paid, and a same-day bot is never that holder. VWRP is shown beside it from 2019.
- Compounded: total = overnight × daytime (the two legs multiply to the whole).
- **Read with care.** The daily OPEN is yfinance's first London price of the day (normally the opening auction).
  On a thin day it can be stale (22 VWRL and 25 VWRP bars have open = close), which moves part of a day's move
  into "overnight". The size of the split is therefore approximate; its sign agrees with published work on many
  markets (most of the long-run stock return arrives overnight). Description only.
