## The pence trap, and why nothing tripped it

Trading 212 and yfinance quote most London shares in **pence** — and they quote
those shares' dividends in pence too. The clean store keeps prices in **pounds**.
Convert the price and forget the dividend and BP's **6.1844p** payout sits next to
the **£5.407** share it came out of: an apparent **114.4% yield**, every quarter,
for every London payer — exactly 100× the real 1.14%.

(An earlier version of this page put 112% beside 1.14%. Those were two different
quarters: 112% was the August payment against an August price, 1.14% the May one.
Same payment both ways, the ratio is exactly 100×, which is the whole point.)

So the conversion happens once, at the boundary, to both — and then the result is
checked against the price it came out of before it is allowed in. A single payment
worth more than 15% of the share price is marked **SUSPECT** and kept for a human:
not silently accepted (which would hide a units error) and not dropped (which would
lose a real special dividend).

On the real data it reads as it should:

| | dividend | share price | yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| BP.L | £0.0619 | £5.41 | 1.14% a quarter |
| SHEL.L | £0.2918 | £32.39 | 0.90% |
| AAPL | $0.27 | $292.68 | 0.09% |

## Two accumulating ETFs, and two I refused to guess about

An accumulating fund reinvests instead of paying out, so having no dividends is
correct, not missing data. I only mark a fund `ACC: N/A` when **two** things agree:
no distributions in its entire history **and** its own name says it accumulates.
Thirteen qualify.

`SGLN.L` and `SSLN.L` — physical gold and silver — have no payouts but their names
say nothing about accumulating. They are listed as **UNKNOWN** rather than counted
either way. They almost certainly hold metal and have no income at all, but "almost
certainly" is not evidence.

## Return in pounds — and the mistake that makes everything look better

There is now one function for P3's number. On real holdings over the last year:

```
BP.L    price alone +31.63%   dividends £0.2506   TOTAL in pounds +37.55%
AAPL    price alone +28.46%   currency effect +2.00%   TOTAL +30.88% gross, +30.81% net
VWRP.L  price alone +16.94%   no dividends (accumulating)   TOTAL +16.94%
```

**It refuses to use "Adj Close".** yfinance offers that column with dividends
already reinvested; adding our dividend table to it counts every payout twice. The
result would look entirely reasonable — just better than the truth, always, by
roughly the yield. The function raises rather than returning a flattering number,
and the clean store never saved the column in the first place.

**Each payment is converted at the rate on its own ex-date**, not today's. A
dividend paid in March met a different exchange rate from one paid in September.

**Withholding tax is a stated assumption, not a hidden constant:** US 15% *assuming
a W-8BEN form is on file* (30% without one), UK 0%. Neither is confirmed for this
account, which is why both the gross and the net figure are always shown.

The arithmetic is pinned by three hand-worked examples — a US payer with a currency
move, a UK payer in pence, and a non-payer — each matching to the penny. A fourth
test plants the pence/pounds error and proves the calculation turns red: £0.0625
gives +11.25%, and the same number left in pence gives +135%.

## A rule about minute data — and what it is NOT

The 1-minute census is 100% for US names and 55% for UK shares. That gap is **not
missing data**: a thinly-traded London share has no bar in a minute when nothing
changed hands, and never will. Handing both kinds of series to the same strategy is
how a model learns a pattern that only exists in the gaps.

So every name now carries a label — `MINUTE_OK` or `FIVE_MIN_ONLY` — and asking for
minute bars on a `FIVE_MIN_ONLY` name is **refused** at the point of reading.

- The cautious label is the **default**: new names, names with fewer than 10 sessions
  of minute history, and anything unknown.
- **Demotion is immediate** on a failed census — the data has already changed.
- **Promotion needs two consecutive passing censuses**, so a label cannot flip on
  one quiet afternoon.

**Right now every one of the 221 names is `FIVE_MIN_ONLY`, including the 98 US names
that passed.** That is the rule working, not a fault: only one census has run, and
promotion needs two. The second runs tomorrow in the after-hours step. I could have
run the census twice today to promote them; two runs over the same data is not two
pieces of evidence, so I did not.

**This changes no gate.** PLAN_V3's 95% bar and the way it is measured are exactly
as written — the census still counts all 221 names, and the label is reported beside
that number, never inside it. A test pins the denominator, because a usage rule that
could shrink a gate's denominator would be a back door around the gate.

|---|---|---|
| 1-minute | **167 of 221 pass (75.6%) — RED** | 0 MINUTE_OK, 221 FIVE_MIN_ONLY |
| 5-minute | **217 of 221 pass (98.2%) — GREEN** | — |

## Is S3 done? Not quite — one thing, and it is only waiting

All five exit-gate items are green, and the whole ingest list the stage asks for is
now in: prices, dividends, currency rates, earnings dates.

**What is left is the quote delay.** The stage says it "measures the quote delay that
row o could not", and row o still cannot be quoted: **1 session of the 3 required for
the US, and none at all for London.** There is nothing to build — the recorder
simply has to run on Monday and Tuesday. I have also marked the first figures as
indicative only: they were taken before yesterday's sampling fix, so they include
readings from a backfill, where "the newest bar" is the end of a 60-day historical
fetch rather than the live feed.
