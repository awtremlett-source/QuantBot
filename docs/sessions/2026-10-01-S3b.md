## The one command for you to run

The recorder is scheduled in a way that silently loses data on a laptop. Paste this
into **PowerShell** (no administrator needed) and press enter:

```powershell
Set-ScheduledTask -TaskName 'QB2-Recorder' -Settings (New-ScheduledTaskSettingsSet -AllowStartIfOnBatteries -DontStopIfGoingOnBatteries -StartWhenAvailable -MultipleInstances Queue -ExecutionTimeLimit (New-TimeSpan -Hours 3))
```

**Why it matters.** I found four settings working against us, by checking why the
quote delay still had no samples:

- it **will not start** while you are on battery;
- it is **killed** the moment you unplug the charger;
- a run missed because the laptop was asleep is **never made up**;
- a run now takes **more than an hour**, so the next hourly trigger is **refused** —
  that is the error `4320` recorded against today's 20:55 run.

Minute-by-minute prices can only be fetched for about 30 days afterwards. Every day
the recorder does not run is a day we can never test on. The command above lets it
run on battery, survive unplugging, catch up after a missed run, and queue rather
than refuse.

I did not run it myself — it changes a Windows setting, and those are yours.

## What I did

**Checked every name we record against Trading 212's own list** — which the new key
can finally read. This was supposed to be routine and was not. Four of our names
were wrong, and one of them is the kind of error that could have run for a year
unnoticed:

- **`AHT.L` is not Ashtead any more.** Trading 212's `AHTl_EQ` is now *Sunbelt
  Rentals*, on a US ISIN. The ticker survived a change of company. A price chart
  cannot show you this; only comparing the company identity can.
- **`IEUR.L` does not exist** on Trading 212's London list at all.
- **`IGLN.L` and `SGLN.L` are the same fund** (ISIN `IE00B4ND3602`), one priced in
  dollars and one in pence. We were holding it twice and calling it diversification.
- **`RXRX`** trades $55m a day against the $800m the bot needs.

And the trap underneath all of it: **Trading 212's ticker is a historical name**. It
keeps whatever a company was called when it was listed, so Meta is `FB_US_EQ`, RTX
is `UTX_US_EQ` (United Technologies), NatWest in London is `RBSl_EQ` (Royal Bank of
Scotland). 39 of our names are affected. It matters because `NWG_US_EQ` *also*
exists — NatWest's **New York** listing, in dollars. Software that builds a ticker
out of the letters it knows would have bought the wrong share, in the wrong
currency, on the wrong continent, at prices that look perfectly normal. Nothing
constructs a ticker any more; every name is looked up and recorded with its ISIN.

**Widened the list from 119 names to 226**, by measurement rather than by
familiarity: US names must trade at least $800m a day, UK is the whole FTSE 100,
ETFs must be sterling-quoted and trade at least £6m a day with one line per fund.
Recording all 226 costs **5 MiB a weekday** — about 1.2 GiB a year. Disk was never
the constraint.

**Proposed the bot's universe**: 50 names in three tagged sleeves. It says
**PROPOSED**, which means not agreed and not tradable, and the S4 gate now refuses
to open while it does. See the decision below.

**Built the front door** — the one and only way data gets into the clean store. It
converts pence to pounds exactly once, refuses to run twice at the same time,
treats its index as the commit point so a power cut cannot leave half a file, and
reconciles exactly: 1,393,651 rows in, 1,393,651 accounted for, nothing unexplained.

**Counted what we actually have** (the census) and **drew the first two charts**
from the clean store.

## DECISION REQUESTED — agree the bot's universe, or don't

The bot's list of 50 names is **proposed**. Until you agree it, no back-test may run
on it. **If you say nothing, nothing happens** — that is the safe default.

The three sleeves, and what a round trip costs before any profit:

| sleeve | names | cost 1× | at 2× stress |
|---|---|---|---|
| London ETFs | 10 | **0.20%** | 0.40% |
| US shares | 30 | **0.40%** | 0.80% |
| FTSE 100 shares | 10 | **0.70%** | 1.40% |

UK shares are three and a half times more expensive than ETFs because **stamp duty
takes 0.5% of every buy**. The bot trades same-day, so it pays the whole round trip
within a day: a UK share signal must clear 0.7% just to break even. I have included
the UK share sleeve **deliberately, as the expensive arm**, so that the plan's P17
("decide the market by test") is settled by measurement rather than by my opinion.
I expect it to lose. Details in `docs/universe/README.md`.

## Two defects found in our own cost model

Both were invisible because the tests and the code shared one wrong assumption —
they agreed with each other and disagreed with the broker. Feeding them Trading
212's real vocabulary exposed both:

- **Stamp duty was never being charged.** The model asked whether an instrument's
  kind was `"SHARE"`; Trading 212's data says `"STOCK"`. The largest single cost in
  the model silently became zero on every UK buy — every UK back-test would have
  looked 0.5% per trade better than reality.
- **Pence were being treated as foreign currency.** A London share quoted in pence
  was charged a 0.15%-per-leg conversion fee to turn pounds into pounds, wrongly
  condemning 97 of the 100 FTSE names.

Wrong in both directions at once. Both now have tests written against the broker's
own words, proven to fail on the old code first. And an unrecognised instrument kind
is now an error rather than a quiet zero — a cost model that stops is worth more
than one that is quietly optimistic.
